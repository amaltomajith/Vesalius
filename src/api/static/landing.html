Open Phantom How it works

A full-body anatomy atlas with a real head CT on its shoulders. Scroll, and follow that scan through the pipeline. Drag to look around.

01

Received, untouched

The scan arrives as a stack of slices and is stored exactly as received. It is catalogued, then checked: the slices are ordered by where they sit in the body, not by their numbers. A broken series is quarantined, never repaired.

S1 Ingest · S2 Index · S3 Quality gate

02

Names removed

Names, IDs and dates are stripped from every header, and so are private vendor fields and any text burned into the pixels. A second, independent pass checks that nothing survived. Unverified data goes no further.

S4 De-identify · S5 Verify, a hard gate

03

The face comes off

The slices become one volume, and the face is cut away before any surface exists. A head without a verified cut never reaches 3D, however clean its tags are.

S6 Volume · S7 Deface, a hard gate

04

Every structure, named

Each organ, bone and vessel is found and named, meshed on its own, and exported with its measurements and the record of every step that made it.

S8 Segment · S9 Surface · S10 Mesh · S11 Export

05

Served, and proven

Only the gold copy is ever served, through a read-only role that cannot see anything else. Five automated proofs try to break these promises, on this machine.

What the pipeline will not do

Data

  • Rewrite bronze. Raw files are write-once and content-addressed.
  • Drop a record. Rejected series are quarantined with a reason and a row.
  • Serve anything but gold. Original IDs live in a schema the serving role cannot read.

Geometry

  • Warn instead of refuse. The defacing gate fails closed.
  • Repair bad geometry. No interpolated slices, no forced spacing.
  • Forget how it got here. Every output traces back through recorded, versioned runs.
